## Notes

### Video 1: The Essence of Calculus

**Key Concepts:**

- Think about the area of a circle: area = πR². Where does this formula come from?
- Trying to find its area, we can think of the idea of slicing the circle into many concentric rings - this respects the symmetry of the circle
- Say the radius of the full circle is 3
- And say that one of those rings has some inner radius r that is between 0 and 3
- If we can find a nice way to get the area of each ring like this one, and a nice way to add them all up, we can get the full circle's area
- We can imagine straightening out this ring, and approximate it as a rectagle
- Its width is the circumference of the original ring 2πr
- Its thickness depends on how finely we chopped up the circle, so let's call it a variable dr for a tiny diffrence in the radius from one ring to the next; perhaps we can think about it as something like dr = 0.1
- So approximating this unwrapped ring as a thin rectacnge, its area is 2πr * dr, or 2πrdr
- For smaller and smaller choices of dr, this will be a better and better approximation for that area, since the top and bottom sides of this shape will get closer and closer to being exactly the same length
